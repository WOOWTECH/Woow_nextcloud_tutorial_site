Chapter 10

Photos

Photos in Nextcloud lets you browse your pictures like a cloud photo album, create albums, and pair it with automatic backup from the mobile app. By the end of this chapter, your photos will have a timeline, albums and (depending on your version) organization by people and places.

Photos are more than files

Photos sitting in Files are only a pile of files. Through the Photos app they line up on a timeline by capture time, you can gather them into albums, and you can find them by place and by person. Add automatic backup from your phone and you have a self-hosted photo album service.

Chapter task: get to know the timeline and albums in Photos, and see how automatic phone backup hooks up. Browsing only, read-only throughout.

How Photos relates to Files

Photos is not another storage space; it "presents the photos already in Files as albums". The photos themselves still sit in a folder under Files (commonly /Photos or your phone backup folder).

Photos featureWhat it means
TimelineSorted by capture time, newest first
AlbumsA set of photos you gather by hand (the original files stay where they are)
FavoritesEvery photo you have marked, in one place
Places / PeopleDepending on your version and settings, organized by map and by face
Dates come from EXIF: photos are sorted by the capture time embedded in the file, not by when they were uploaded. A date that looks wrong usually means the EXIF data is missing.

Five steps to start using Photos

  1. Open Photos

    Click "Photos" in the top bar. The timeline is what you see by default.

  2. Browse the timeline

    Scroll down for older photos, click a thumbnail to open it full size, and use the arrow keys to move between them.

  3. Create an album

    On the left, "Albums" → add a new one, name it, then put photos in it (this does not move the original files).

  4. Mark favorites

    Mark a photo as a favorite, and you can find all of them at once under "Favorites" on the left.

  5. Connect automatic phone backup

    Install the mobile app (Chapter 19) and turn on auto upload. New photos from your phone go into Nextcloud on their own and show up in Photos.

Photo privacy

  • Your photos live on your own server, and AI / face recognition (if you turn it on) runs locally too. Nothing goes out.
  • Sharing an album is the same as sharing files (Chapter 8): a link is a key, so set a password and an expiry.
  • Keep sensitive photos in a folder with restricted access, or do not share them at all.

Common photo pitfalls

  1. A photo is missing from Photos

    Check that the photo is in a folder Photos scans; something you uploaded a moment ago may still need time for thumbnails and indexing.

  2. Thumbnails keep spinning

    Indexing a large batch for the first time takes a while; it smooths out once the server has finished its background jobs (Chapter 21).

  3. The dates are in the wrong order

    Usually the EXIF capture time is missing or wrong, which is a problem with the photo's own metadata.

  4. No People / Places

    These features depend on your version and on whether the matching app / setting is enabled, so not every server has them.

Photos FAQ

What is the difference between Photos and keeping photos in Files?
The photos themselves are all in Files. Photos only presents them as albums and a timeline, which is a more convenient way to browse and organize them.
Do photos from my phone come in automatically?
They do once you install the mobile app and turn on auto upload (Chapter 19). After that, anything you shoot is backed up automatically over Wi-Fi.
Does creating an album copy the photos and take up twice the space?
No. An album is a collection, so the same photo can sit in several albums and still take up space only once.
Can I share a whole album?
Yes, it works like sharing a folder or a collection: a link with a password and an expiry (Chapter 8).
What happens when I delete a photo?
Deleting puts it in the trash bin (Chapter 6), and you can restore it until the retention period runs out. Removing a photo from an album only takes it out of that album; the original file stays.
