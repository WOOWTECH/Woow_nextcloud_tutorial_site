Chapter 7

Upload files and folders

Move files and whole folders from your computer into Nextcloud. By the end of this chapter, you'll be able to bring in large batches with drag and drop and the upload button, read the progress display, and know how to retry when an upload fails.

Move your stuff in first

Nextcloud only starts to pay off once your files are gathered in one place. Document backups, photos, an entire project folder — whatever it is, learn to upload reliably first, and the sharing, syncing and collaboration that follow all have something to stand on.

Chapter task: bring files and folders in with drag and drop and the upload button, and learn to read the progress display and handle failures.

Two ways to upload

MethodHowBest for
Drag and dropDrag files or folders from your computer onto the Files screenMoving a lot at once, or a whole folder
The upload buttonClick "+" → "Upload file / folder", then pick what you wantPicking exact files, or if drag and drop is not for you
Switch to the target folder first: an upload lands in whichever folder you are currently in. Go to the right place before you upload and you save yourself moving things later.

Six steps to upload

  1. Open the target folder

    In Files, click into the folder where the files should go (for example "Documents").

  2. Drag and drop, or use the upload button

    Drag the files onto the page; or click "+" → "Upload file", and use "Upload folder" to bring in a whole folder at once.

  3. Watch the upload progress

    The page shows the upload progress. Large files and large batches take time, so do not close the tab.

  4. Handle files with the same name

    If a file with the same name is already there, you are asked whether to "Keep both" or "Overwrite". Read the prompt before you choose.

  5. Confirm it finished

    Once the progress finishes and the files appear in the list, the upload worked.

  6. Spot-check the files

    Open a few of them and check that the contents are complete and the sizes look right.

Do not close the browser mid-upload: the upload happens live, and closing the tab interrupts whatever has not finished. Wait for the progress to finish before you leave.

Large files and large batches

  • Lots of small files: "Upload folder" is faster than picking them one by one, but a large number of small files still takes a while.
  • A single large file: videos and backup files may run into the site's per-file size limit; if a file is over it, ask your admin to adjust it (Appendix B).
  • A more reliable route: for large or regular transfers, desktop sync (Chapter 18) or WebDAV (Chapter 20) is steadier than uploading through the browser.

Common upload pitfalls

  1. The upload fails halfway

    Check that your network is stable, then try again. Large files fail more often, so upload in batches or switch to the desktop client.

  2. It says the file is too large

    You are over the site's per-file limit. Ask your admin to adjust the upload size limit (Appendix B).

  3. Out of space

    Your quota or the server's disk is full. Empty the trash bin, delete duplicates, or ask your admin to raise your quota.

  4. Only part of a folder uploaded

    It was probably interrupted. Upload again; for files with the same name you will be asked whether to overwrite or keep both.

Upload FAQ

Can I upload a folder?
Yes. Use "+ → Upload folder", or drag the folder straight onto the page; subfolders come along with it.
Does uploading compress or change my files?
No. Nextcloud stores files exactly as they are: it does not alter the contents and it does not compress them.
What is the largest single file I can upload?
It depends on the upload limit configured on the site. If you are over it, ask your admin to adjust it (Appendix B lists the relevant settings).
Can I delete the originals on my computer after uploading?
Confirm everything uploaded successfully first, keep a backup if the files matter, and only then delete the local originals.
Is there a more reliable way to upload than the browser?
Yes. The desktop sync client (Chapter 18) and WebDAV (Chapter 20) suit large or regular uploads better.
