Chapter 9

Manage and revoke shares

Creating a share takes seconds; keeping it under control does not. By the end of this chapter, you'll be able to take stock of every share, change permissions, disable links and revoke access, so that "convenient" never turns into "over-exposed".

A share you cannot take back is a problem

A share link is a key you have lent out. If you never take stock of what you lent and never revoke anything, before long you have no idea how many people can still see your files. Reviewing your shares regularly is one of the most important security habits in a self-hosted cloud.

Chapter task: take stock of what you have shared and what other people have shared with you, then change permissions and disable the links you no longer need. Throughout, you only tighten access; you delete no files.

The share lists

In Files, under the "Shares" category on the left, you will usually see:

ListWhat is in it
Shared by youThe files and links you have shared with other people
Shared with youFiles other people have shared with you
Shared by linkThe public links you have created
PendingShare invitations you have not accepted yet

Five steps to review and revoke

  1. Open "Shared by you"

    In Files, go to "Shares → Shared by you / Shared by link" on the left and see what is currently open to the outside.

  2. Check them one by one

    Open the sharing panel on a file and look at who each share is with, its permissions, its password and its expiration date.

  3. Change the permissions

    Set anything that should not be editable back to read-only, and add an expiration date to anything that has no expiry.

  4. Disable or delete the link

    Remove a link you no longer need straight from the sharing panel; it stops working immediately.

  5. Check "Shared with you"

    Remove anything other people have shared with you that you never use, and keep the list clean.

Build a rhythm: review your shares once a month, and for a link you shared temporarily with a supplier or for an event, revoke it the moment you are done.

The minimal-exposure principle

  • Share only the files or folders that genuinely need sharing, never your whole file library.
  • Put a password and an expiry on an external link by default.
  • Share to an account on this site rather than a public link whenever you can (the other person has to log in, which is safer).
  • Check the "Shared by link" list regularly and revoke the ones you forgot about.
If a link leaks: disable that link immediately, generate a new one if you still need it, and work out how far the leak reached.

Common management pitfalls

  1. You cannot find a share

    Use the "Shared by link" list, or search for the file name; a share hangs off the file itself.

  2. You disabled it and they can still see it

    They may already have downloaded a copy. Disabling only blocks further access; anything already downloaded cannot be taken back.

  3. You want to revoke everything at once

    You have to work through them one at a time. Revoke the important ones first, and clear out the temporary ones as a priority.

  4. The other person keeps getting notifications

    Adjust the notification settings, or remove the share.

Share management FAQ

After I disable a link, is the other person completely locked out?
They can no longer reach it through that link, but a copy they downloaded earlier cannot be taken back. So share sensitive content with care.
Can I see who has downloaded something?
The file's "Activity" logs events such as access and downloads, which gives you something to audit against.
Can I remove something someone shared with me?
You can remove the share on your own side, and it does not affect their original file.
How do I revoke a group share?
Remove that group in the sharing panel and the whole group loses access together.
How often should I review my shares?
Once a month is a good rhythm. If you set up a temporary share, revoke it the moment you are done.
