Chapter 11

Calendar

Use Calendar in Nextcloud to create events, set reminders, share calendars, and sync with the calendar on your phone or computer. By the end of this chapter, your family or your team will have one shared calendar that you control.

Run your own calendar

A calendar is the heart of how a family or a team coordinates. With Nextcloud Calendar your event data stays at home, you can still share calendars and sync across devices, and you never hand your schedule to a third-party cloud.

Chapter task: create a calendar and an event, set a reminder, and learn how sharing and syncing work.

Calendars, events and sync protocols

ConceptWhat it means
CalendarA set of events; you can keep several (work, family, birthdays)
EventOne item on your schedule, with a time, a place, reminders and attendees
CalDAVThe standard protocol for calendar sync; it is what your phone and computer sync through
Subscription (ICS)A read-only subscription to an external calendar (public holidays, for example)

Five steps to start using Calendar

  1. Open Calendar

    Click "Calendar" in the top bar or the Apps menu. Your list of calendars is on the left.

  2. Create a new calendar

    On the left, "+ New calendar". Name it (for example "Family") and pick a color so you can tell them apart.

  3. Create an event

    Click a day cell and fill in the title, time and place. You can mark it "all-day" or make it repeat.

  4. Set reminders and attendees

    Inside the event, add a reminder (a set number of minutes before) and add attendees by email to send invitations.

  5. Share or subscribe

    Share the calendar with an account on this site or through a link. You can also "subscribe" to an external ICS calendar (read-only).

Sync across devices: add an account to the calendar on your phone or computer with the CalDAV URL (you can copy it from the settings), and your events stay in two-way sync.

Sharing and subscribing

  • Share with an account on this site: they can see (or edit) your calendar, which suits a family sharing one.
  • Public link: generate a read-only link for people without an account.
  • Subscribe to an external calendar: paste the ICS URL for public holidays, a sports team's schedule and the like, and it shows up read-only in your calendar.

Common calendar pitfalls

  1. Times are off by an hour

    The time zone is not set correctly. Confirm your time zone in personal settings (Chapter 5) so event times come out right.

  2. The phone will not sync

    Check the CalDAV URL and your credentials (an app password is recommended), or set it up with the official app.

  3. No reminder went off

    Check that the event actually has a reminder and that your notification settings are on (Chapter 5), and that your phone allows notifications too.

  4. Editing one recurring event changed them all

    When you edit a recurring event, look carefully at whether you are changing "this one" or "the whole series".

Calendar FAQ

Can I sync with Google Calendar?
You can connect the two through CalDAV, or by subscribing to / importing an ICS file. The setup is on the advanced side, so go by the official docs for the details.
Can I have several calendars?
Yes. Use different colors for work, family, birthdays and so on, and each one can be shared separately.
Can the people I share a calendar with edit it?
It depends on the permissions you give. You can set read-only or can edit; for a family calendar, can edit is the common choice.
Can I edit an external calendar I subscribe to?
No, a subscription is read-only. To edit, it has to be your own calendar.
Which app should I use on my phone?
Add a CalDAV account to your system calendar, or use the matching official Nextcloud app. Both iOS and Android support CalDAV.
