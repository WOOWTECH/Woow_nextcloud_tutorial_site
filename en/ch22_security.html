Chapter 22

Security and troubleshooting

Work through one systematic security and maintenance check, from trusted domains and HTTPS to backups and common troubleshooting. By the end of this chapter, you'll know how to narrow a problem down layer by layer, and which security habits are worth holding on to for the long run.

Stay calm, work in layers, do not just restart

When your cloud goes wrong, the most common mistake is to try your luck with "let me restart it" and "let me change a setting", which usually washes the clues away. The right move is to look at the symptom first, work down through the layers, and act only once you have found the evidence. Security, meanwhile, comes down to a few habits you keep up over the long term.

Chapter task: learn the security checklist and the layered troubleshooting routine.

Trusted domains (trusted_domains)

Nextcloud only accepts connections for the domains on its list. When you expose it on a custom URL, that URL has to be added to trusted_domains, or you get "Access through untrusted domain".

If you see untrusted domain: nothing is broken. The URL is not on the trusted list. That is an admin setting (Appendix B): add the domain in the add-on settings or in config.php.

Six-step security checklist

  1. The entry point is https

    The address bar shows https and a padlock. If you get a certificate warning, stop there and do not type your account or password.

  2. Strong passwords and two-factor

    Make passwords long enough and do not reuse them; turn on two-factor authentication for admin accounts.

  3. Take stock of the accounts

    Do not leave an account enabled that should be disabled, and demote the admins you do not need (Chapter 21).

  4. Take stock of the shares

    Disable the share links you no longer need, and set a password and an expiry on public links (Chapter 9).

  5. Check connected devices

    In personal settings, under "Security", revoke any app password or session you do not recognize (Chapter 5).

  6. Confirm the backups

    The admin confirms that the database and the data directory are backed up on a schedule, and that a restore drill has actually been run.

Backup and restore

The worst thing that can happen to a self-hosted cloud is something going wrong with the server. A complete backup has to cover three parts:

What to back upWhat it is
DatabaseStructural data: users, shares, metadata and so on
Data directoryThe file contents themselves
Config fileSite settings such as config.php
Verify your backups: a backup you have never restored does not count. Run a restore drill on a schedule and confirm you really can get everything back.

Troubleshooting layer by layer

  1. Establish the scope first

    Ask "is it only me or is it everyone", so you know whether this is one device or the whole site.

  2. Access through untrusted domain

    The URL has not been added to the trusted domains; that is an admin setting (Appendix B).

  3. Cannot connect / 502

    The server may be restarting or mid-update, or the Tunnel may be down. Wait a moment and try again, or ask your admin to check the service status.

  4. Out of space, uploads fail

    Empty the trash bin, delete duplicates, check the quota; if it comes to it, the admin can add storage.

  5. Previews or search do not update

    Background jobs are not running properly (Chapter 21). Switch to Cron mode.

  6. Login is not working

    Rule out the password, two-factor and the certificate one at a time; if anything looks suspicious, change the password and revoke the devices.

Security and troubleshooting FAQ

Is self-hosting really safer than the cloud?
You hold the data, but you also carry the responsibility for keeping it safe. The privacy advantage of self-hosting only holds once you stay on top of three things: accounts, shares and backups.
How do I fix "untrusted domain"?
Add the public URL to trusted_domains (in the add-on settings or config.php). That is an admin job (Appendix B).
Should I turn on two-factor authentication?
Strongly recommended, especially for admin accounts. It is the single most effective step you can take for account security.
How often should I back up?
It depends on how often your data changes: daily or weekly at the least, and verify a restore on a schedule.
What should I include when I report a problem?
The time, what you did, the error message (redact anything sensitive) and what it affects (only you or everyone). The more complete it is, the easier it is to deal with.
