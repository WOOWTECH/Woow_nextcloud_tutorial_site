Chapter 5

Accounts and personal settings

Change your password, switch the language, and set your display name and personal details. By the end of this chapter, you'll have your account in a state that is both comfortable and safe, and you'll know what each tab in personal settings covers.

Get your account in shape first

Personal settings is the control panel for your own account: password, language, notifications, theme and connected devices all live here. Spend five minutes getting it right and everything afterwards feels much safer — password security most of all.

Chapter task: open personal settings, change your password, and confirm your language and display name. These affect only your own account, nobody else's.

The tabs in personal settings

Open personal settings from the "settings menu" behind your avatar at the top right. Several tabs run down the left:

TabWhat it covers
Personal infoDisplay name, email, phone, avatar, language, time zone
SecurityChanging your password, devices and sessions, app passwords, two-factor authentication
NotificationsWhich events notify you, and how
Appearance and accessibilityTheme, dark mode, font

Five steps to set up your account

  1. Open personal settings

    Click your avatar at the top right → choose "Settings" to land on the personal settings page.

  2. Change your password

    Go to the "Security" tab and enter your old password and the new one. Make it long, and do not reuse it on any other service.

  3. Confirm your language and time zone

    In "Personal info", set the language to the one you want (English on this site) and set the time zone to your own, so your calendar and the times it shows are correct. Note: Calendar and Tasks show the wrong times until this matches where you actually are.

  4. Set your display name and avatar

    Fill in a display name and upload an avatar, so other people can recognize you in shares and chats.

  5. Check your notification preferences

    Go to the "Notifications" tab, turn off what you do not need and keep what matters (shares, mentions).

What an app password is: the "Security" tab lets you create an app password for the desktop client, the mobile app and WebDAV (Chapters 18-20). That way your main password never has to be typed all over the place.

Account security recommendations

  • Strong password: long and not reused, especially on an admin account.
  • Two-factor authentication: if the site supports it, turn it on. It raises your account security a great deal.
  • Check connected devices: the "Security" tab lists the devices currently logged in and the app passwords you have issued. Revoke the ones you do not recognize.
  • Log out on a public computer: when you are done, log out from the avatar menu and close the browser.
Revoke anything you do not recognize: if you see a device or an app password in "Security" that you cannot account for, revoke it at once and change your main password.

Common settings pitfalls

  1. The password change is rejected

    It probably fails the password policy (too short, too common). Pick a longer, more complicated combination.

  2. You changed the language and nothing changed

    Refresh the page. Some text only picks up the new language after you log in again.

  3. You cannot find a settings tab

    Some tabs appear only once the matching app is enabled. Admin settings sit behind a separate "Administration settings" entry (Chapter 21).

  4. You lost your two-factor device

    Log in with a backup code. Without one, ask your admin to disable two-factor authentication for the account.

Personal settings FAQ

What is the difference between personal settings and Administration settings?
Personal settings affect only your own account. Administration settings, which only admins can see, affect the whole site; see Chapter 21.
Do I have to set up an app password?
Not necessarily, but you should. Giving a third-party client an app password lowers the risk of your main password leaking, and it can be revoked on its own.
Who can see my avatar?
Other users on the same site see your display name and avatar when you share, chat or work on a shared file.
Can I change which app I land in by default?
Some versions let you set in personal settings which app you land in after you log in (Files instead of Dashboard, for example).
Can I still change my password if I have forgotten the old one?
Changing your password requires the old one. If you have genuinely forgotten it, ask your admin to reset it (Chapter 21).
