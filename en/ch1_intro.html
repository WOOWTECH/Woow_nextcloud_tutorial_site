Chapter 1

Before you start: keep your cloud at home

First, get clear on what Nextcloud is, why you would self-host a cloud, and the account roles and public exposure to settle before you start. By the end of this chapter, you'll know what this Nextcloud setup can do for you, and which lines you must not cross.

Why keep your cloud at home

Most people's files — documents, photos, address books, calendars — are scattered across cloud services: Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud. Those services are convenient, but a few things about them should give you pause: storage costs a monthly fee, your data sits in someone else's data center, and when a service shuts down or changes its rules, you have no choice but to follow.

Nextcloud moves the whole "cloud drive + collaboration platform" package back into your own home. Your files live on your own server, and from a browser or an app you upload, share, sync and collaborate the way you would on any cloud drive — the difference is that the data is in your hands.

Chapter goal: not to teach you how to set up a server, but to make sure that before you start using it you understand what this Nextcloud setup looks like, what the account roles are, and where the security boundaries sit. The hands-on work starts with the install in Chapter 2.
To be clear: half of the "security" of a self-hosted cloud is your own responsibility. A weak account password, or share links handed out carelessly, will defeat even the best architecture. This guide comes back to "minimal exposure" again and again.

What Nextcloud is

Nextcloud is a self-hosted cloud collaboration platform. It covers the same ground as the cloud services you already know, and you can extend it:

CapabilityWhat Nextcloud offersWhen you would use it
File storage and sharingFiles: uploads, folders, share links, permissionsBacking up documents, sending big files to family and friends
Photo backupPhotos: auto upload, timeline, albumsGetting phone photos home automatically
Calendars and contactsCalendar, ContactsA shared family calendar and address book
Communication and collaborationTalk (chat and video), Office (documents), Deck (boards)Video meetings, documents online, project management
Sync across devicesDesktop client, mobile app, WebDAVFiles syncing automatically between computer and phone
In one line: Nextcloud is a self-hosted "Google Drive + Google Photos + Calendar + Meet" rolled into one, and Apps extend it as far as you want.

  • Login: an admin account or a standard user account, with a password (Chapter 3).
  • Home: after you log in you land on Files by default, and the Apps menu at the top left switches to Photos, Calendar, Talk and the rest (Chapter 4).
  • Extending it: the Apps store adds more features (Appendix A).
In one line, the architecture: you (browser or app) → Cloudflare Tunnel (an encrypted tunnel) → Nextcloud on the WoowTech server. Nothing in between needs a public port open, and there is no IP address to remember.

Decide first: account roles and public exposure

Before you start, two ideas decide how safe everything you do afterwards will be: who has an account, and what is public.

RoleWhat they can doWho it suits
AdminManage users, storage, Apps and site-wide settingsWhoever maintains the server (usually WoowTech)
Standard userUpload, organize and share their own filesFamily members, everyday use

Public exposure has three common settings, from the safest to the most open:

  1. Nothing public

    Only people with an account can see anything, and even the entry point requires you to authenticate first. This is the default, and the safest way to use it.

  2. Share only specific files or folders

    Use the share link from Chapter 8 to open one file or folder to family and friends; nobody sees anything else of yours.

  3. Wide-open public access

    Exposing your whole file library or the admin interface to the internet. This guide does not recommend it unless you understand the risk very well.

Hard stop: do not expose the admin interface, account passwords or your whole file library for the sake of "convenience". When someone needs to see something, share that one file or folder, and you can still revoke it when they are done.

Security boundaries before you start

Security in Nextcloud does not end once it is set up. It is a handful of lines you hold every time you use it:

  • Account passwords: use a long password you do not reuse anywhere else, and all the more so for the admin account. Do not keep passwords in a notes app or a group chat.
  • HTTPS: the site URL always starts with https://. If the browser warns you that it is "not secure", stop there and do not type your account details.
  • Share links: a link you hand out is a key, and whoever holds it can see what it opens. Give it only to the people who need it, and control it with a password and an expiry date.
  • Minimal exposure: make public only the files that have to be, never the whole site, and do not hand admin-area functions to people who have no need for them.
Rule of thumb: for any decision about public exposure, ask yourself first: "if a stranger saw this, could I live with it?" If the answer is no, tighten it.

Before you start FAQ

What is the difference between Nextcloud and Google Drive?
The experience is much the same, but Nextcloud files live on your own (or WoowTech's) server rather than in Google's data center. You hold control of the data, and with it the responsibility for backups and maintenance.
Do I need to know how to write code or set up a server?
Not as a user. You need to be able to open a browser and use an app. WoowTech takes care of deployment and maintenance; this guide teaches you how to use it.
Can other people see my files?
By default, people see only their own files, and only after logging in. Unless you create a share link or make a folder public yourself, nothing is visible from outside. Hold to the security checks in Chapter 22 as you go.
What do I do when storage runs out?
Check your usage first, then clean up: remove duplicates, empty the trash bin, archive old files. When you genuinely need more room, ask your admin to handle storage, and do not delete server files yourself.
The site URL will not open, or shows as not secure. What now?
First check the URL is https://woowtech-nextcloud.woowtech.io (mind the https and the full spelling). If the browser shows a certificate warning, stop and contact your admin; do not click past the warning and log in anyway.
