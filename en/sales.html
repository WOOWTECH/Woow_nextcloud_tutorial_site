Why your home needs a self-hosted cloud

These days your data is scattered across cloud services: files in Google Drive, photos in iCloud, the calendar somewhere else entirely. Convenient, but you pay a monthly fee, your data sits in someone else's data center, and when the service changes its rules you have to go along with it.

Nextcloud moves the whole set — "cloud drive + albums + calendar + collaboration" — back into your own house. The interface is as good to use as the ones you have now; the difference is that the data is in your hands.

The one-line pitch: "A cloud service is renting someone else's warehouse; a self-hosted cloud is building your own. Either way the contents are yours — the difference is who holds the key."

    FAQ

    Can anyone else see my files?
    With Option A, the data sits on your own machine. For Option B, WoowTech's administrative-access model is an open product question. In either option, only grant user-facing share access intentionally.
    How does this compare with Google Drive?
    One Nextcloud service covers files, albums, calendar and collaboration. Data location, maintenance responsibility, storage limits and charges differ by deployment option and must be confirmed before purchase.
    Do I need to know how to set up a server?
    No. As a user you only need to open a web page and install an app; setup and maintenance responsibilities are described in the option comparison above.
    What if I run out of space?
    Option A capacity depends on your hardware. Option B storage limits, expansion process and charges require owner confirmation.
    Do phone photos back themselves up automatically?
    Yes. Install the app, turn on auto upload, and each new photo uploads to your configured Nextcloud service over Wi-Fi.
