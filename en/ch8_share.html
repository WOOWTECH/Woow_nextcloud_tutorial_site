Chapter 8

Share files and folders

Share a single file or folder with someone safely: create a link, set a password and an expiry, and control the permissions. By the end of this chapter, you'll be able to let someone view or work on exactly the content you pick, without handing over your account.

Sharing hands over a key, not the door

The worst way to get a file to someone is to give them your account password — that hands over your entire cloud. The right way is a share link: it opens only the one file or folder you meant to open, and you can still put a password, an expiry and permissions on it. Learn to use it and sharing stops turning into leaking.

Chapter task: create a share link for one file or folder, and set its permissions and expiry. Sharing means opening something to the outside, so work out how far that reach goes before you start.

Who you share with, and what they can do

Who you share withMethodRisk
A user or group on this siteShare straight to their accountLow: they still have to log in
External (no account)Create a public share linkMedium: a link is a key, so keep control of it

The usual permission options are read-only (view and download only), can edit (change files and upload), and File drop (upload only, and they cannot see anyone else's files).

A link is a key: once a public link gets out, anyone holding it can reach the content within whatever permissions you set. Before you share, make sure the content holds nothing that must not leak.

Six steps to share something

  1. Pick the file or folder to share

    Find it in Files and click the share icon on the right (or "⋯ → Share") to open the sharing panel.

  2. Choose a recipient, or create a link

    On this site: type their account or group. External: click the "+" next to "Share link" to create a public link.

  3. Set the permissions

    Choose read-only, can edit or File drop. Only use can edit when you are sharing a folder for someone to work in.

  4. Set a password and an expiry

    In the link settings, add a password and set an expiration date. Always set an expiry on a temporary share.

  5. Copy the link and send it

    Copy the link, send it over a channel you trust, and tell the recipient not to pass it on.

  6. Plan to revoke it when you are done

    Keep a note of this share; once you no longer need it, go to share management and disable it (Chapter 9).

Hard stop: do not post a share link to a public forum or a group chat. If something genuinely has to stay on public display, assess that risk separately.

How to set up common sharing scenarios

ScenarioRecommended settings
Send a document for family or friends to readPublic link, read-only, with an expiry
Collect photos or assignments from a groupA folder with File drop (upload only)
Edit alongside a colleagueShare to their account on this site, can edit
A download open to the publicRead-only, password-protected, and check the content is not sensitive

Common sharing pitfalls

  1. They say the link will not open

    Check that the link is complete, has not expired and has not been disabled; if you set a password, send them that too.

  2. There is no "Share link" option

    Your admin may have turned off public link sharing, or you may not have share permissions on that file.

  3. They cannot upload

    The permission has to be "can edit" or "File drop"; read-only cannot upload.

  4. You shared more than you meant to

    Go to share management in Chapter 9, take stock, and disable the shares you no longer need.

Sharing FAQ

Can I put an expiry on a share link?
Yes, and it is strongly recommended for anything temporary. Once the date passes, the link stops working on its own.
Does the other person need a Nextcloud account?
Not for a public link. If you share to an account on this site, they do have to be able to log in.
Can I let someone upload without seeing anyone else's files?
Yes, use the "File drop" permission. It suits collecting assignments or photos.
Can I still change a file after I have shared it?
Yes. You are the owner, so you can change the content or revoke the share whenever you want.
How do I tell who has accessed it?
The file's "Activity" and the sharing panel show the relevant records; Chapter 9 covers the detail.
